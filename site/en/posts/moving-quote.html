Moving Quotes in Korea: On-site vs Remote

Updated July 2026·7 min read
In brief

Remote quotes often become more expensive on moving day when the actual volume and access differ. Use an on-site estimate and write the truck, crew, schedule, scope, equipment, insurance, and surcharge conditions into the quote.

On this page

  1. How to collect comparable quotes
  2. Reference ranges as of May 2026
  3. Five common extra charges
  4. Verify before booking

How to collect comparable quotes

  1. Request on-site estimates from three to five licensed companies.
  2. Record truck size: 1, 1.5, 2.5, or 5 metric tons.
  3. Record the number of crew members and whether any work is subcontracted.
  4. Write the departure and arrival windows.
  5. List air conditioners, washers, pianos, safes, built-ins, disposal, and reassembly.
  6. State whether elevators, stairs, lift trucks, long carries, and parking fees are included.
  7. Write every surcharge condition and the cargo-insurance limit and deductible.

These are market-practice references, not fixed prices. Peak dates and weekends can add roughly 30–50%, and lift-truck charges vary by height, access, and time.

Five common extra charges

  • A larger truck because the actual volume exceeds the estimate.
  • Stair labor where an elevator cannot be used.
  • Distance, fuel, tolls, or a long carry from the truck to the door.
  • Pianos, safes, large appliances, or other specialty items.
  • Wardrobe, bed, or built-in disassembly and reassembly.

Put each applicable item into the estimate before signing. A written fixed scope is the best defense against a moving-day price dispute.
